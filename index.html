What We've Been Working On

A programme of incremental improvements

No single change explains the uplift.
This is what compounding SEO work looks like.

1

Structured data — FAQ & service schema

FAQ and service schema added across key pages, improving how Nick Brookes appears in search results — richer snippets, greater visual presence, stronger relevance signals. A direct contributor to the CTR improvement seen throughout the year.

2

Meta title refresh — sitewide

Title tags refreshed across the site to better reflect search intent and improve click-through from results pages. Homepage clicks are up +38.5% year on year while impressions grew only modestly — a clear signal that the page became more compelling to click, not simply more visible. CTR improved from 5.6% to 6.2%, a measurable uplift that points directly to stronger, more relevant titles pulling more prospective customers through from search.

3

Site speed improvements

Technical performance work completed in the period. Page speed is a confirmed Google ranking signal. The homepage average position improves from 19.4 in January–February to 14.9 in March–May — the timing aligns directly with this work.

4

Homepage redesign

A focused UX improvement programme delivered to the homepage in the period — subtle but high-impact changes including margin alignment, card styling, contrast improvements and CTA legibility. Work of this nature directly influences how users engage with the page once they arrive, and signals quality to Google through improved engagement metrics. Average homepage position improves from 19.4 in the weeks before the work to 14.9 in the weeks after — consistent with Google rewarding improved page experience signals."

5

Skip hire & aggregates content improvements

Content strengthened across skip hire location pages and the aggregates section. The generic /skip-hire/ page grew +157% YoY. Aggregates clicks up +173%. The March content work correlates directly with the acceleration in both traffic and leads from that point forward.

Each of these improvements builds on the last. Schema improves CTR. Better titles improve CTR. Faster pages improve rankings. Better content improves rankings and conversion. The data across this report reflects all five strands working together — and sets the foundation for the content programme that forms the focus of next steps.

Why impressions are up modestly despite strong click growth

The site is now ranking in more visible positions, generating higher-quality impressions that convert to clicks. Lower-quality impressions — where the site appeared far down page 2 or beyond — have reduced.

The "skip hire near me" effect — a positive story

In 2025, Nick Brookes appeared for "skip hire near me" at an average position of 59 — generating 25,743 low-quality impressions that almost never converted to clicks. In 2026, Google's AI Overviews and local pack have taken over that query entirely, reducing organic impressions to 2,885. Crucially, Nick Brookes' ranking has not dropped — if anything it has improved. Google has simply removed the query from organic results for most users. This is an industry-wide shift affecting every skip hire business in the country and accounts for the majority of the raw impressions variance between the two periods.

Average position improving significantly

Sitewide, average position has improved from 29.4 in 2025 to 24.9 in 2026 — a gain of 4.5 positions across every search the site appears in. Among the top 20 ranking queries the improvement is even more pronounced, moving from 7.1 in 2025 to 5.2 in 2026 — shifting the site from the bottom of page 1 to the top half, where click-through rates are substantially higher.

Next Steps

Recommended priorities

Consolidate the gains.
Content is the unlock for the next level.

Sitewide content programme

The data tells a clear story: the site has strong brand authority and is generating significant impression volumes across its service and location pages. The barrier to converting more of those impressions into clicks — and more clicks into leads — is content depth and quality. A sitewide programme to improve the depth, relevance and authority of page content is the single highest-leverage activity available.

1
Location pages — Chester, Northwich, Wrexham, Macclesfield — these pages are losing ground to competitors with genuinely richer content. Rather than expanding the number of location pages, the priority is making the existing ones meaningfully better — incorporating local context, council information, coverage areas, skip sizes and genuine customer reviews from each area. Pages built around real local value consistently outperform templated alternatives.
2
/septic-tanks/ — 93,348 impressions with an average position of 28. This page is appearing in searches but ranking too low to earn clicks. A comprehensive content overhaul would make a material difference to one of the site's highest-potential pages.
3
/concrete/ and /aggregates/ — combined 65k+ impressions at deep positions. Both pages need significantly stronger content to rank competitively for high-value commercial terms in Cheshire and the surrounding area.

Supporting priorities

4
Grab hire section — the hub page has 14,338 impressions at position 39. As with skip hire, individual location pages could extend the reach of this service — but only if they are built with genuinely differentiated local content from the outset rather than using a shared template.
5
Internal linking — as content is improved across service and location pages, a structured internal linking plan will distribute authority from the strong homepage and Crewe pages to the pages that need it most.
6
Location page expansion — only once the existing pages perform — adding new location pages before the underperforming ones are fixed would be the wrong order of priority. Once Chester, Northwich, Wrexham and Macclesfield are recovered and ranking strongly, that's the proof of concept for carefully considered expansion into areas like Stoke, Ellesmere and Warrington.
